# CHAPTER 3

# THE PROBLEM OF SIN

## Key Verse

> "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."
> 
> — Romans 3:23

* * *

# Introduction

Before we can fully appreciate the good news of the Gospel, we must first understand the bad news.

Many people want salvation without understanding why salvation is necessary.

They want forgiveness without understanding sin.

They want a Savior without recognizing their need to be saved.

The Bible teaches that humanity's greatest problem is not poverty, ignorance, injustice, disease, or political conflict.

These are serious problems, but they are symptoms of a deeper issue.

The root problem is sin.

Sin affects every human being.

It damages our relationship with God.

It corrupts our hearts.

It distorts our thinking.

It harms our relationships.

Ultimately, it leads to death.

Until we understand the seriousness of sin, we will never fully appreciate the greatness of God's grace.

The Gospel begins with an honest assessment of the human condition.

Only then can we understand why Jesus came.

* * *

# 1\. WHAT IS SIN?

## Scripture

Romans 3:23

> "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."

Many people define sin differently from the Bible.

Some define sin as merely breaking social rules.

Others define sin as hurting other people.

Still others define sin according to personal preference:

"If it feels wrong to me, it's sin."

The Bible provides a much deeper understanding.

Sin is any thought, attitude, word, or action that falls short of God's holy standard.

Sin includes what we do.

It also includes what we fail to do.

Jesus demonstrated that sin is not merely external behavior.

It begins in the heart.

Anger can become murder in seed form.

Lust can become adultery in seed form.

Pride, selfishness, greed, and unbelief all originate within the human heart.

Sin is ultimately rebellion against God.

It is choosing our own will above His.

# 2\. THE UNIVERSALITY OF SIN

## Scripture

Romans 3:10–12

> "None is righteous, no, not one..."

One of the most difficult truths for people to accept is that sin affects every human being.

Most people compare themselves with others.

They think:

- "I'm not as bad as that person."
- "I've never committed serious crimes."
- "I try to be a good person."

While these statements may be true, they miss the point.

The standard is not other people.

The standard is God.

God is perfectly holy.

Perfectly righteous.

Perfectly pure.

When measured against God's holiness, every person falls short.

This is what Paul means when he writes:

> "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."

Notice the word "all."

There are no exceptions.

Sin affects:

- Rich and poor
- Educated and uneducated
- Religious and non-religious
- Young and old
- Men and women

The Bible does not divide humanity into good people and bad people.

It divides humanity into sinners who recognize their need for God's grace and sinners who do not.

This truth should produce humility.

No one can boast before God.

Every believer stands on equal ground at the foot of the cross.

# 3\. THE CONSEQUENCES OF SIN

## Scripture

Romans 6:23

> "For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord."

Every action has consequences.

A farmer who plants seeds expects a harvest.

An employee who works expects wages.

Paul uses this principle to explain the consequences of sin.

The wage earned by sin is death.

Death in Scripture involves more than physical death.

It includes:

### Spiritual Death

Sin separates humanity from fellowship with God.

### Physical Death

The human body eventually dies because sin entered the world.

### Eternal Death

Those who reject God's provision of salvation remain separated from Him forever.

This is why sin is not a small problem.

Sin is not merely a mistake.

Sin is not merely an imperfection.

Sin carries devastating consequences.

Yet even in this sobering verse, we find hope.

The verse does not end with death.

It continues:

> "But the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord."

The Gospel is God's answer to humanity's greatest problem.

# 4\. SIN SEPARATES US FROM GOD

## Scripture

Isaiah 59:1–2

(Read the passage.)

Many people assume that God feels distant because He has moved away from them.

The Bible teaches the opposite.

The problem is not God's distance from us.

The problem is our separation from Him through sin.

Isaiah explains that God's power has not diminished.

His ability to hear has not weakened.

Rather, sin creates a barrier between humanity and God.

This separation affects every aspect of life.

People often experience:

- Guilt
- Shame
- Fear
- Spiritual emptiness
- Broken relationships
- Lack of peace

These are symptoms of a deeper separation.

Human beings were created for fellowship with God.

When that relationship is broken, something essential is missing.

Throughout history people have attempted to bridge this separation through:

- Religion
- Rituals
- Good works
- Philosophy
- Self-improvement

Yet none of these efforts can remove sin.

Only God can solve the problem that sin created.

This is why the Gospel is necessary.

* * *

2.  Why can't human effort remove sin?
    
3.  How does sin affect our relationships with others?
    
4.  Why is reconciliation with God humanity's greatest need?

# 5\. THE HEART OF THE PROBLEM

## Scripture

Mark 7:20–23

(Read the passage.)

Many people believe that the primary problem with humanity is external.

They blame:

- Society
- Politics
- Education
- Poverty
- Culture

While these factors influence behavior, Jesus points to a deeper issue.

The problem originates within the human heart.

Jesus teaches that sinful actions flow from sinful desires.

Evil actions are symptoms.

The heart is the source.

This truth explains why external solutions alone cannot solve humanity's deepest problems.

Laws can restrain behavior.

Education can increase knowledge.

Governments can improve conditions.

Yet none of these can transform the human heart.

Only God can do that.

The Gospel addresses the root problem, not merely the symptoms.

God does not simply call people to behave differently.

He transforms them from within.

This is why Jesus spoke about being "born again."

Humanity does not merely need improvement.

Humanity needs renewal.

2.  What does Jesus teach about the source of sin?

# 6\. WHY GOOD WORKS CANNOT SAVE US

## Scripture

Ephesians 2:8–9

(Read the passage.)

One of the most common beliefs in the world is that good people go to heaven.

Many assume that if their good deeds outweigh their bad deeds, God will accept them.

The Bible teaches otherwise.

The problem is not that good works are unimportant.

The problem is that good works cannot erase sin.

Imagine standing before a judge after committing a serious crime.

Suppose you say:

"But Judge, I helped my neighbors.  
I gave to charity.  
I volunteered at community events."

Those good actions do not remove guilt.

They are good actions, but they do not cancel wrongdoing.

Similarly, no amount of human effort can remove the guilt of sin before a holy God.

This is why salvation is by grace.

It is a gift.

Good works are the result of salvation, not the cause of salvation.

Christians do good works because they have been saved.

They are not saved because they do good works.

# 7\. WHY WE NEED A SAVIOR

## Scripture

Luke 19:10

> "For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost."

If sin were merely a minor flaw, humanity would need a teacher.

If sin were merely ignorance, humanity would need education.

If sin were merely bad habits, humanity would need self-discipline.

But because sin separates us from God and leads to death, humanity needs a Savior.

This is why Jesus came.

Jesus did not come merely to provide moral instruction.

He came to rescue sinners.

Throughout the Gospels, Jesus repeatedly sought out:

- The lost
- The broken
- The guilty
- The outcast
- The spiritually bankrupt

He came to accomplish what humanity could never accomplish for itself.

The Bible presents Jesus as the only sufficient solution to humanity's deepest problem.

Without Christ, humanity remains lost.

With Christ, forgiveness, reconciliation, and eternal life become possible.

The seriousness of sin helps us understand the greatness of God's love.

The darker the problem, the brighter the Gospel shines.

# CHAPTER SUMMARY

The Bible teaches that sin is humanity's greatest problem.

Sin is any thought, word, action, or attitude that falls short of God's holy standard.

All people have sinned.

No one is exempt.

The consequences of sin include spiritual death, physical death, and eternal separation from God.

Sin separates humanity from fellowship with God and corrupts the human heart.

Because the problem originates within, external solutions are insufficient.

Good works cannot remove sin or earn salvation.

Humanity needs a Savior.

This is why Jesus came.

The bad news of sin prepares us to understand the good news of the Gospel.

Only when we recognize our need can we fully appreciate God's provision in Christ.
