# Introduction

If Chapter 3 reveals humanity's problem, Chapter 4 begins to reveal God's solution.

When people become aware of their sin, they must decide how they will respond.

Some ignore it.

Some excuse it.

Some blame others.

Some experience guilt but never change.

The Bible calls us to something deeper.

It calls us to repentance.

# 1\. WHAT IS REPENTANCE?

## Scripture

Acts 3:19

> "Repent therefore, and turn back, that your sins may be blotted out."

Repentance is one of the most important themes in the Bible.

John the Baptist preached repentance.

Jesus preached repentance.

The apostles preached repentance.

Yet many people misunderstand what repentance means.

Some think repentance simply means feeling sorry.

Others think repentance means performing religious rituals.

Still others view repentance as punishment for wrongdoing.

The biblical concept of repentance is much deeper.

The word "repent" literally means a change of mind that results in a change of direction.

Repentance involves:

- Recognizing sin
- Agreeing with God about sin
- Turning away from sin
- Turning toward God

Repentance is not merely changing behavior.

It begins with a transformed heart and mind.

Imagine driving toward the wrong destination.

The moment you realize your mistake, you make a U-turn.

That is a picture of repentance.

You stop moving away from the desired destination and begin moving toward it.

Spiritually, repentance means turning away from self-rule and turning toward God's rule.

It is not perfection.

It is a change of direction.

The repentant person may still struggle, stumble, and fail at times, but the overall direction of life has changed.

# 2\. REPENTANCE IS MORE THAN REMORSE

## Scripture

Matthew 27:3–5

(Read the passage.)

One of the clearest examples of remorse without repentance is Judas Iscariot.

After betraying Jesus, Judas experienced deep regret.

He recognized the seriousness of his actions.

He even returned the money he had received.

Yet Scripture never describes Judas as truly repentant.

He felt remorse.

He did not turn to God.

This distinction is important.

Many people feel bad about sin.

They experience:

- Shame
- Regret
- Embarrassment
- Fear of consequences

Yet none of these emotions necessarily lead to repentance.

A person may regret being caught without regretting the sin itself.

A person may feel sorrow over consequences while continuing to love the behavior that caused them.

True repentance goes beyond emotion.

It produces a changed heart and a changed direction.

Repentance is not measured merely by tears.

It is measured by transformation.

The question is not:

"Do I feel bad?"

The question is:

"Am I willing to turn to God?"

# 3\. GODLY GRIEF VS. WORLDLY GRIEF

## Scripture

2 Corinthians 7:10

> "For godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation without regret, whereas worldly grief produces death."

Paul distinguishes between two kinds of sorrow.

### Worldly Grief

Worldly grief focuses on self.

It says:

- "I got caught."
- "I ruined my reputation."
- "I am suffering consequences."
- "People think less of me."

Worldly grief is primarily concerned with personal loss.

It may produce sadness.

It may produce tears.

But it does not necessarily produce repentance.

### Godly Grief

Godly grief focuses on God.

It recognizes that sin is ultimately an offense against Him.

King David expressed this after his sin with Bathsheba:

> "Against you, you only, have I sinned and done what is evil in your sight."
> 
> — Psalm 51:4

Godly grief produces humility.

It produces confession.

It produces repentance.

It produces a desire to be restored to fellowship with God.

One kind of sorrow leads to life.

The other leads only to despair.

The difference is not the intensity of emotion.

The difference is the direction of the heart.

# 4\. ZACCHAEUS: A PICTURE OF REPENTANCE

## Scripture

Luke 19:1–10

(Read the passage.)

Zacchaeus was a chief tax collector.

In his day, tax collectors were often associated with corruption and greed.

Many people viewed them as traitors and sinners.

When Zacchaeus heard that Jesus was coming, he was eager to see Him.

Because he was short in stature, he climbed a sycamore tree to catch a glimpse of Jesus.

To everyone's surprise, Jesus stopped and invited Himself to Zacchaeus' home.

This encounter changed Zacchaeus' life.

His response demonstrates genuine repentance.

Zacchaeus declared:

> "Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor. And if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold."
> 
> — Luke 19:8

Notice what happened.

Jesus did not force Zacchaeus to make restitution.

His transformed heart produced transformed behavior.

Repentance led to action.

This is an important principle.

Good works do not produce repentance.

Repentance produces good works.

When the heart changes, behavior follows.

Zacchaeus became a living example of the power of God's grace.

# 5\. REPENTANCE AND TRANSFORMATION

## Scripture

Romans 12:1–2

(Read the passage.)

Many people think repentance is a one-time event.

In reality, repentance is both an initial response to the Gospel and an ongoing part of the Christian life.

Martin Luther famously wrote:

> "When our Lord and Master Jesus Christ said 'Repent,' He intended that the entire life of believers should be one of repentance."

As disciples grow, the Holy Spirit continually reveals areas that need transformation.

These may include:

- Pride
- Selfishness
- Anger
- Bitterness
- Greed
- Impurity
- Unbelief

Repentance becomes a lifestyle.

Not because Christians live in constant guilt.

But because they desire continual growth.

Repentance is not merely turning away from sin.

It is turning toward Christ.

As believers behold Christ, they are gradually transformed into His image.

This process continues throughout life.

# 6\. WHY REPENTANCE IS NECESSARY FOR SALVATION

## Scripture

Mark 1:14–15

(Read the passage.)

At the beginning of His public ministry, Jesus proclaimed:

> "Repent and believe in the gospel."

Notice that repentance and faith are closely connected.

They are like two sides of the same coin.

Repentance turns away from sin.

Faith turns toward Christ.

One cannot truly embrace Christ while refusing to turn from sin.

Nor can one genuinely turn from sin without turning to Christ.

Repentance does not earn salvation.

Salvation remains entirely a gift of God's grace.

However, repentance is the appropriate response to God's grace.

A heart that refuses repentance demonstrates that it has not yet fully understood the Gospel.

The Gospel calls us not only to believe certain facts about Jesus.

It calls us to surrender our lives to Him.

Repentance is not the cause of salvation.

It is evidence that God's work has begun in the heart.

# CHAPTER SUMMARY

Repentance is a change of heart and mind that results in a change of direction.

It involves turning away from sin and turning toward God.

Repentance is more than remorse, regret, or fear of consequences.

True repentance flows from a heart that recognizes sin and desires reconciliation with God.

Godly sorrow produces repentance and life.

Worldly sorrow produces only regret and death.

The story of Zacchaeus demonstrates that genuine repentance results in practical transformation.

Repentance is not merely a one-time event at conversion.

It becomes an ongoing lifestyle for every disciple of Jesus.

Repentance and faith work together in responding to the Gospel.

Repentance turns away from sin.

Faith turns toward Christ.

Both are essential responses to God's grace.
