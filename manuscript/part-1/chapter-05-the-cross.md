# Introduction

The cross stands at the very center of the Christian faith.

Without the cross, there is no Gospel.

Without the cross, there is no forgiveness.

Without the cross, there is no salvation.

Many people view the cross merely as a religious symbol.

Some wear it as jewelry.

Others display it in churches or homes.

Yet the cross represents something far greater.

It is the place where God's justice and God's love meet.

# 1\. WHY DID JESUS HAVE TO DIE?

## Scripture

Romans 5:6–11

(Read the passage.)

One of the most important questions in Christianity is:

Why did Jesus have to die?

Could God not simply forgive sin without the cross?

Could He not overlook humanity's failures and extend mercy?

To answer these questions, we must understand two fundamental truths about God:

- God is perfectly holy.
- God is perfectly loving.

Because God is holy, He cannot ignore sin.

Because God is loving, He desires to save sinners.

The cross is where these two realities meet.

If God ignored sin, He would not be just.

If God punished sinners without providing a way of salvation, His love would not be fully displayed.

At the cross, God demonstrated both His justice and His love.

Jesus did not die because He was a victim of unfortunate circumstances.

He died because it was God's plan to redeem humanity.

From the beginning, God intended to provide a Savior who would bear the penalty for sin and reconcile people to Himself.

The cross was not an accident.

It was God's rescue mission.

# 2\. THE JUSTICE OF GOD

## Scripture

Romans 3:23–26

(Read the passage.)

Many people appreciate God's love.

Far fewer appreciate His justice.

Yet both are essential.

Justice means that wrongdoing must be addressed.

A judge who ignores crime is not considered loving.

He is considered corrupt.

Likewise, God's justice requires that sin be judged.

Throughout Scripture we see God's commitment to righteousness and justice.

Sin matters because God is holy.

Every act of rebellion, every lie, every act of selfishness, every injustice ultimately offends God's perfect character.

The seriousness of sin is measured not only by the act itself but also by the One against whom it is committed.

When people minimize sin, they often minimize God's holiness.

The cross reminds us that sin is far more serious than we often realize.

If forgiveness could have been achieved by any other means, the death of Christ would not have been necessary.

The cost of the cross reveals the seriousness of sin and the holiness of God.

# 3\. THE LOVE OF GOD

## Scripture

Romans 5:8

> "But God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us."

The cross reveals not only God's justice but also His love.

Human love often depends upon the worthiness of the person being loved.

People naturally love those who love them.

God's love is different.

God loved us while we were still sinners.

He loved us before we deserved it.

He loved us while we were still in rebellion.

He loved us when we were spiritually dead.

The cross demonstrates that God's love is not merely an emotion.

It is sacrificial action.

Jesus willingly entered our broken world.

He endured rejection, suffering, humiliation, and death.

Not because He had sinned.

Not because He deserved punishment.

But because He loved us.

Every time we look at the cross, we should remember:

The cross is God's declaration of love toward undeserving sinners.

# 4\. SUBSTITUTIONARY ATONEMENT

## Scripture

Isaiah 53:4–6

(Read the passage.)

At the heart of the Gospel is a profound truth:

Jesus died in our place.

The Bible teaches that Christ became our substitute.

The penalty we deserved was placed upon Him.

This is often called **substitutionary atonement**.

The word "atonement" refers to the work by which reconciliation between God and humanity is accomplished.

The word "substitutionary" means that Jesus stood in our place.

Imagine a person standing before a judge, guilty and unable to pay an enormous debt.

Then someone else steps forward and pays the debt in full.

That is only a faint picture of what Christ accomplished.

Jesus bore the judgment that sinners deserved.

He absorbed the penalty of sin.

He satisfied the demands of divine justice.

Because Christ took our place, believers can be forgiven and reconciled to God.

The cross is not merely an example of love.

It is an accomplishment of salvation.

Jesus did not simply show us how to live.

He did for us what we could never do for ourselves.

# 5\. THE GREAT EXCHANGE

## Scripture

2 Corinthians 5:21

(Read the passage.)

Many theologians describe the Gospel as "The Great Exchange."

At the cross, something remarkable occurred.

Our sin was placed upon Christ.

His righteousness is credited to those who trust Him.

This does not mean believers suddenly become morally perfect.

Rather, it means they are declared righteous before God because of Christ.

Think about the magnitude of this exchange.

Jesus receives:

- Our guilt
- Our shame
- Our condemnation

Believers receive:

- Forgiveness
- Acceptance
- Reconciliation
- Righteousness

This exchange is entirely an act of grace.

No one earns it.

No one deserves it.

No one contributes to it.

Salvation is God's gift.

The Gospel is not primarily about what we do for God.

It is about what God has done for us through Christ.

# 6\. IT IS FINISHED

## Scripture

John 19:30

> "It is finished."

These words are among the most significant ever spoken.

As Jesus hung on the cross, He declared:

"It is finished."

He did not say:

"I am finished."

He said:

"It is finished."

The work of redemption had been completed.

The debt of sin had been paid.

The sacrifice had been offered.

The way to God had been opened.

Nothing more needed to be added.

This is why Christians do not attempt to earn salvation through good works.

Christ has already accomplished everything necessary.

Believers do not work for salvation.

They work from salvation.

The cross stands as a declaration that Christ's sacrifice is sufficient.

No additional payment is required.

No human effort can improve upon what Christ has already accomplished.

# 7\. THE RESURRECTION AND VICTORY OVER DEATH

## Scripture

1 Corinthians 15:1–8

(Read the passage.)

The story does not end at the cross.

Three days after His crucifixion, Jesus rose from the dead.

The resurrection is not an optional part of Christianity.

It is essential.

Without the resurrection:

- The Gospel is incomplete.
- Sin remains undefeated.
- Death remains unconquered.
- Faith is futile.

The resurrection confirms that Christ's sacrifice was accepted.

It demonstrates His victory over sin, Satan, and death.

It validates His claims about His identity.

The resurrection also provides hope for believers.

Because Christ lives, His followers have the promise of eternal life.

The resurrection transforms the cross from a symbol of defeat into a symbol of victory.

Death no longer has the final word.

Jesus does.

# CHAPTER SUMMARY

The cross stands at the center of God's plan of salvation.

Because God is holy, sin must be judged.

Because God is loving, He provided a Savior.

Jesus willingly died in the place of sinners, bearing the penalty that humanity deserved.

Through His substitutionary death, forgiveness and reconciliation became possible.

At the cross, God's justice was satisfied and God's love was displayed.

Christ accomplished everything necessary for salvation.

His declaration, "It is finished," reminds believers that redemption is complete.

The resurrection confirms Christ's victory over sin and death and provides hope for all who trust Him.

The cross reveals both the seriousness of sin and the greatness of God's love.

It remains the foundation of the Christian faith.
