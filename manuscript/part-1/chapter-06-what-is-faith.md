# CHAPTER 6

# WHAT IS FAITH?

## Key Verse

Hebrews 11:6

> "And without faith it is impossible to please him..."

* * *

# Introduction

If the cross reveals what God has done for us, the next question is:

How do we receive what Christ has accomplished?

The Bible's answer is clear:

By faith.

Faith lies at the very heart of Christianity.

Every believer speaks about faith.

Every Christian values faith.

Yet many people misunderstand what faith actually is.

Some think faith means wishful thinking.

Others think faith means believing something without evidence.

Still others think faith is merely optimism or positive thinking.

The Bible presents a very different picture.

Biblical faith is a confident trust in God based upon His character, His promises, and His revelation.

Faith is not believing without evidence.

Faith is responding appropriately to the evidence God has already provided.

Throughout Scripture, men and women of faith trusted God even when circumstances were difficult, uncertain, or seemingly impossible.

Faith enabled them to obey God, persevere through trials, and experience His faithfulness.

Without faith, no one can have a relationship with God.

Without faith, no one can receive salvation.

Without faith, no one can truly follow Christ.

For this reason, every disciple must understand what biblical faith is and how it transforms life.

* * *

# 1\. WHAT IS FAITH?

## Scripture

Hebrews 11:1

(Read the passage.)

Hebrews provides one of the clearest descriptions of faith in the Bible.

Faith involves confidence.

Faith involves assurance.

Faith involves trust.

Faith is not blind optimism.

Nor is it wishful thinking.

Faith is confidence in the reliability of God.

Every day people exercise faith.

They sit in chairs trusting they will hold their weight.

They board airplanes trusting the engineers and pilots.

They undergo surgery trusting doctors.

Life requires trust.

Biblical faith is trust directed toward God.

It is confidence that God is who He says He is and that He will do what He has promised.

Faith does not remove uncertainty about every detail of life.

Rather, it provides confidence in the One who holds our lives in His hands.

Faith is not confidence in ourselves.

Faith is confidence in God.

# 2\. FAITH IS MORE THAN BELIEF IN FACTS

## Scripture

James 2:19

> "You believe that God is one; you do well. Even the demons believe—and shudder!"

Many people assume that faith simply means believing certain facts.

For example:

- God exists.
- Jesus lived.
- Jesus died on the cross.
- Jesus rose from the dead.

These facts are essential.

However, merely believing these facts is not saving faith.

James points out a startling truth.

Even demons possess correct theology.

They know God exists.

They recognize Christ's authority.

They understand spiritual realities.

Yet they are not saved.

Why?

Because genuine faith involves more than intellectual agreement.

Faith involves trust.

A person may believe a chair exists.

Trust is demonstrated when he sits in it.

Likewise, a person may believe Jesus is the Savior.

Saving faith occurs when he entrusts himself to Christ.

Christianity is not merely believing information about Jesus.

It is placing one's life into His hands.

# 3\. SAVING FAITH

## Scripture

Ephesians 2:8–9

(Read the passage.)

The Bible teaches that salvation is received through faith.

Faith is the means by which we receive God's gift of grace.

Faith itself does not save.

Christ saves.

Faith simply receives what Christ has accomplished.

Imagine a starving person being offered bread.

The bread provides nourishment.

The hand merely receives it.

Likewise, Jesus provides salvation.

Faith receives Him.

Saving faith includes three essential elements:

### Knowledge

A person must understand the Gospel.

### Belief

A person must believe the Gospel is true.

### Trust

A person must personally rely upon Christ.

All three elements are necessary.

Knowledge without trust is insufficient.

Belief without surrender is incomplete.

Saving faith rests entirely upon Jesus Christ and His finished work.

It abandons self-reliance and embraces dependence upon God.

# 4\. FAITH AND TRUST

## Scripture

Proverbs 3:5–6

(Read the passage.)

One of the clearest biblical pictures of faith is trust.

Trust means relying upon God even when we do not fully understand our circumstances.

Many people trust God when life is easy.

Faith becomes more visible when life becomes difficult.

When illness comes.

When finances become uncertain.

When prayers seem unanswered.

When suffering enters our lives.

Faith asks:

"Will I continue trusting God?"

Biblical faith does not deny reality.

It acknowledges difficulties honestly while continuing to trust God's wisdom, goodness, and sovereignty.

Faith says:

"I do not understand everything, but I trust the One who does."

This kind of trust develops through relationship.

The more we know God, the easier it becomes to trust Him.

# 5\. FAITH AND WORKS

## Scripture

James 2:14–26

(Read the passage.)

One of the most misunderstood topics in Christianity is the relationship between faith and works.

The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is by grace through faith.

At the same time, it teaches that genuine faith produces obedience.

Good works do not save us.

Good works reveal that faith is alive.

Imagine a fruit tree.

The fruit does not make the tree alive.

The fruit demonstrates that the tree is alive.

Likewise, good works demonstrate the reality of faith.

A person who claims faith but shows no evidence of transformation should examine whether that faith is genuine.

True faith produces:

- Obedience
- Love
- Compassion
- Generosity
- Holiness
- Perseverance

These actions do not earn salvation.

They flow from a transformed heart.

Faith alone saves.

But saving faith is never alone.

It produces fruit.

# 6\. THE FAITH OF ABRAHAM

## Scripture

Genesis 12:1–4; Romans 4

(Read the passages.)

Abraham is often called the father of faith.

God called Abraham to leave his homeland and travel to a place he had never seen.

Abraham obeyed.

God promised Abraham descendants even though he and Sarah were advanced in age.

Abraham believed.

God promised that through Abraham all nations would be blessed.

Abraham trusted.

Abraham was not perfect.

He experienced failures, fears, and moments of weakness.

Yet the overall pattern of his life was trust and obedience.

This is why Scripture repeatedly points to Abraham as an example of faith.

Faith does not require perfection.

Faith requires trust.

The life of Abraham reminds us that faith is often demonstrated through obedience before we see the outcome.

Faith trusts God's promises even when circumstances seem impossible.

# 7\. GROWING IN FAITH

## Scripture

Romans 10:17

> "So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ."

Faith is not static.

It can grow.

Jesus frequently challenged His disciples regarding the strength of their faith.

At times He described them as having "little faith."

Yet over time their faith matured.

How does faith grow?

### Through God's Word

Faith grows as we learn God's promises.

### Through Prayer

Faith deepens through communion with God.

### Through Obedience

Each step of obedience strengthens trust.

### Through Trials

Difficulties often become opportunities for faith to mature.

### Through Fellowship

Other believers encourage and strengthen our faith.

Spiritual growth rarely occurs instantly.

It develops over time.

The goal is not perfect faith.

The goal is growing faith.

Even small faith placed in a great Savior can accomplish much.

# CHAPTER SUMMARY

Faith is a confident trust in God based upon His character and promises.

It is more than intellectual agreement with facts.

It involves personal reliance upon Jesus Christ.

Salvation is received by grace through faith.

Faith does not earn salvation but receives God's gift.

True faith produces obedience and spiritual fruit.

The life of Abraham demonstrates that faith trusts God even when circumstances appear impossible.

Faith grows through Scripture, prayer, obedience, trials, and fellowship.

The Christian life begins by faith and continues by faith.

Every disciple is called to trust God more deeply each day.
