# CHRISTIANS VS. DISCIPLES

## Knowledge Is Not Enough

Imagine that I wanted to teach my daughter how to play basketball.

As a father, I would want to give her every possible advantage. I might buy the best books ever written on basketball. Together we could watch NBA games and instructional videos from the world's best coaches. We could study the rules of the game, learn offensive and defensive strategies, and analyze the techniques of great players.

After months of study, she might know a tremendous amount about basketball. She could explain the rules. She could describe how to dribble, pass, shoot, and defend. She might even score highly on a written examination about the game.

But then comes the real test.

One day we walk onto a basketball court. I hand her the ball and say, "Now dribble."

The ball bounces off her foot.

"Try again."

This time she loses control of the ball.

"Now shoot."

The ball misses the basket completely.

After several attempts she becomes discouraged. Perhaps she even begins to cry.

"Dad," she says, "I've studied so hard. Why am I so bad at basketball?"

The answer is simple.

She has been taught basketball.

She has not been trained in basketball.

Knowing about basketball and playing basketball are not the same thing.

No amount of reading can replace practice. No amount of watching videos can develop muscle memory. A player becomes skilled only through training, repetition, correction, discipline, and experience.

The difference between knowledge and practice is the difference between information and transformation.

## Teaching and Training

This principle applies directly to the Christian life.

Many believers spend years attending church services, listening to sermons, reading Christian books, and studying the Bible. These activities are valuable and necessary. We cannot follow Christ if we do not know what He taught.

However, discipleship involves more than acquiring information.

A disciple does not merely learn what Jesus taught.

A disciple learns to obey what Jesus taught.

The Scriptures are given not only for teaching but also for training. God's Word shapes our minds, but it is also meant to shape our lives. Every truth we learn is intended to be lived out in practice.

A person who studies basketball without ever stepping onto a court will never become a basketball player.

Likewise, a person who studies Christianity without putting Christ's teachings into practice will never mature as a disciple.

This is why James warns believers not to deceive themselves by merely hearing the Word. The goal is not simply to listen. The goal is to obey.

Discipleship begins with learning, but it does not end there. True discipleship involves practicing what we learn until Christ's character is formed in us.

## From Information to Transformation

Many churches do an excellent job teaching people the Bible. Yet Jesus never commanded His followers merely to transfer information.

He called them to make disciples.

A disciple is a learner, but more than a learner. A disciple is a follower. A disciple is an apprentice. A disciple orders his life around the teachings and example of his Master.

Jesus did not say:

>"Go and make students who know My teachings."

He said:

>"Go and make disciples."

The difference is profound.

Students accumulate knowledge.

Disciples undergo transformation.

Students master information.

Disciples learn obedience.

Students graduate from a course.

Disciples spend a lifetime following their Master.

The question therefore is not simply:

>"What do I know about Jesus?"

The more important question is:

>"Am I becoming like Jesus?"

2. What is one teaching of Jesus that you know but need to obey more consistently?

3. How can you move from merely learning about Christ to actively following Him this week?

# 1\. WHAT IS A DISCIPLE?

## Scripture

Matthew 4:19

> "Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men."

The word disciple appears throughout the New Testament.

A disciple is more than a student.

A disciple is more than a believer.

A disciple is a learner, follower, and apprentice.

In the first century, disciples followed a teacher closely, learned from his example, adopted his values, and sought to become like him.

This is precisely what Jesus calls people to do.

Notice Jesus' invitation:

> "Follow me."

Christianity is fundamentally about a relationship with a Person.

Jesus did not merely provide information.

He called people to follow Him.

A disciple learns:

- What Jesus taught
- How Jesus lived
- What Jesus valued
- How Jesus loved
- How Jesus served

The goal of discipleship is not merely knowledge.

The goal is Christlikeness.

A disciple increasingly becomes like the One he follows.

# 2. CHRISTIAN OR DISCIPLE?

## Scripture

Acts 11:26

> "...and in Antioch the disciples were first called Christians."

Many believers are surprised to learn that the word Christian appears only a few times in the New Testament, while the word disciple appears hundreds of times.

Before believers were called Christians, they were known as disciples.

This observation is important because it reveals how the early church understood the Christian life.

The New Testament does not present two categories of believers:

- Ordinary Christians
- Serious Disciples

Rather, every Christian was expected to be a disciple.

Every disciple belonged to Christ.

Every believer was called to follow Jesus.

Unfortunately, over time many people began to think of Christianity primarily as an identity.

A person might say:

> "I am a Christian."

What they often mean is:

- I attend church.
- I believe in God.
- I agree with Christian teachings.
- I identify with Christianity.

While these things may be good, Jesus called people to something much deeper.

He called them to follow Him.

A disciple is not merely someone who believes certain truths about Jesus.

A disciple is someone who orders his life around Jesus.

A disciple seeks to learn from Jesus, imitate Jesus, obey Jesus, and become like Jesus.

The question therefore is not:

> "Do I call myself a Christian?"

The more important question is:

> "Am I following Jesus?"

## A Helpful Comparison

| Christian (Cultural Understanding) | Disciple (Biblical Understanding) |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Identifies with Christianity | Follows Jesus |
| Knows about Christ | Learns from Christ |
| Attends church occasionally | Lives under Christ's lordship |
| Seeks information | Pursues transformation |
| Receives | Also serves |
| Believes | Believes and obeys |

This table is not intended to create two classes of believers.

Rather, it highlights the difference between merely identifying with Christianity and actively following Christ.

The New Testament expectation is that every Christian becomes a disciple.

## Am I Following Jesus or Merely Admiring Him?

Many people admire Jesus.

They admire:

- His compassion
- His wisdom
- His courage
- His teaching

The crowds admired Jesus.

But not everyone who admired Him followed Him.

Many listened to His teaching.

Far fewer surrendered their lives to Him.

Admiration is not discipleship.

Agreement is not discipleship.

Church attendance is not discipleship.

Discipleship begins when a person responds to Jesus' invitation:

> "Follow Me."

# 3\. JESUS' CALL: FOLLOW ME

## Scripture

Matthew 9:9

(Read the passage.)

Throughout the Gospels, Jesus repeatedly extended the same invitation:

> "Follow me."

He said it to fishermen.

He said it to tax collectors.

He said it to ordinary men and women.

His invitation was simple.

Yet it was life-changing.

When Jesus called Matthew, Matthew left his tax booth and followed Him.

When Jesus called Peter, Andrew, James, and John, they left their nets and followed Him.

Following Jesus involved a radical reordering of priorities.

Jesus became more important than:

- Career
- Wealth
- Comfort
- Reputation
- Personal ambition

This does not necessarily mean abandoning one's occupation.

It means placing Christ above every competing loyalty.

Discipleship begins when Jesus becomes Lord of every area of life.

# 4\. COUNTING THE COST

## Scripture

Luke 14:25–33

(Read the passage.)

Jesus never hid the cost of discipleship.

In fact, He often emphasized it.

He compared discipleship to:

- Building a tower
- Going to war

In both examples, people must count the cost before proceeding.

Jesus wanted potential followers to understand what discipleship involves.

Following Christ may require:

- Sacrifice
- Perseverance
- Courage
- Commitment
- Obedience

The Gospel is free.

Discipleship costs everything.

Salvation is a gift.

Following Jesus requires surrender.

This does not mean earning salvation.

Rather, it means recognizing that Christ deserves complete allegiance.

The question is not:

"What will discipleship cost me?"

The deeper question is:

"What is Christ worth?"

When we understand who Jesus is and what He has done, the cost of discipleship becomes worthwhile.

# 5\. DENY YOURSELF AND TAKE UP YOUR CROSS

## Scripture

Luke 9:23

> "If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me."

This verse contains one of the clearest descriptions of discipleship.

Jesus identifies three requirements:

### Deny Yourself

This does not mean self-hatred.

It means rejecting self-rule.

The disciple no longer places personal desires at the center of life.

Christ becomes the center.

### Take Up Your Cross Daily

In the Roman world, the cross symbolized death.

Jesus calls disciples to die to self-centered living.

This is not a one-time decision.

It is a daily choice.

### Follow Me

Discipleship is not merely avoiding sin.

It is actively following Christ.

The Christian life involves continual surrender, obedience, and trust.

Every day believers face choices.

Will they follow their own desires?

Or will they follow Jesus?

Discipleship is choosing Christ again and again.

# 6\. LORDSHIP AND OBEDIENCE

## Scripture

Luke 6:46

> "Why do you call me 'Lord, Lord,' and not do what I tell you?"

The title "Lord" was one of the earliest Christian confessions.

To declare Jesus as Lord was to acknowledge His authority.

Yet Jesus warns against empty profession.

A person may claim allegiance to Christ while refusing His authority.

True discipleship involves obedience.

Obedience is not legalism.

It is the natural response of love.

Jesus said:

> "If you love me, you will keep my commandments."
> 
> — John 14:15

Obedience does not create a relationship with Christ.

Obedience flows from a relationship with Christ.

The disciple asks:

- What has Jesus commanded?
- How can I obey Him?
- How can I honor Him today?

The goal is not perfection.

The goal is faithful submission.

# 7\. THE MARKS OF A DISCIPLE

## Scripture

John 13:34–35

(Read the passage.)

How can we recognize a disciple?

The New Testament identifies several characteristics.

### Love

Jesus said the world would recognize His disciples by their love.

### Obedience

Disciples seek to obey Christ's teachings.

### Fruitfulness

Disciples produce spiritual fruit.

### Perseverance

Disciples continue following Christ despite difficulties.

### Humility

Disciples serve rather than seek status.

### Dependence Upon God

Disciples recognize their need for God's grace.

No disciple displays these qualities perfectly.

However, they should become increasingly evident over time.

Spiritual maturity is not measured merely by knowledge.

It is measured by transformation.

# 8\. MAKING DISCIPLES

## Scripture

Matthew 28:18–20

(Read the passage.)

Jesus did not simply command His followers to be disciples.

He commanded them to make disciples.

This responsibility belongs to every believer.

Disciple-making involves:

- Sharing the Gospel
- Teaching God's Word
- Encouraging spiritual growth
- Modeling Christian living
- Helping others follow Christ

Many Christians assume disciple-making is reserved for pastors or church leaders.

The New Testament presents a different picture.

Every disciple is called to influence others.

Disciples make disciples.

This principle has fueled the growth of Christianity for two thousand years.

The Christian life is not merely about receiving.

It is also about reproducing.

Healthy disciples help others become disciples.

# CHAPTER SUMMARY

Jesus calls people not merely to believe in Him but to follow Him.

A disciple is a learner, follower, and apprentice of Jesus Christ.

The New Testament presents discipleship as the normal Christian life.

Following Jesus involves surrender, obedience, perseverance, and love.

Disciples count the cost, deny themselves, take up their cross daily, and submit to Christ's lordship.

Spiritual maturity is demonstrated through transformed character and faithful obedience.

Finally, disciples make disciples.

The goal of discipleship is not merely personal growth but participation in Christ's mission.

Every disciple is called to help others follow Jesus.
