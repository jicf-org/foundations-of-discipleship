# THE GREAT COMMISSION

## Key Verse

Matthew 28:18–20

> "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations..."

* * *

# Introduction

The Christian life does not end with salvation.

It does not end with baptism.

It does not end with joining a church.

It does not even end with spiritual growth.

Every disciple is called to participate in God's mission.

Throughout this workbook we have followed a journey:

We sought God.

We learned His Word.

We understood sin.

We repented.

We trusted Christ.

We became disciples.

We joined His Church.

We learned to walk in the Spirit.

We fixed our hope on Christ's return.

Now one final question remains:

What should we do until He returns?

Jesus answered that question clearly.

We are called to make disciples.

The final command of Jesus reveals the ongoing mission of every believer.

The Christian life is not merely about receiving blessings.

It is about participating in God's redemptive work in the world.

* * *

# 1\. THE GREAT COMMISSION

## Scripture

Matthew 28:18–20

(Read the passage.)

These verses are among the most important in the entire New Testament.

After His resurrection, Jesus gathered His disciples and gave them their final instructions.

Notice the command:

> "Make disciples."

The primary command is not:

- Go
- Baptize
- Teach

Those actions support the main command.

The central mission is making disciples.

A disciple is a follower of Jesus.

Therefore disciple-making involves helping others become followers of Jesus.

The Great Commission remains the mission of the Church today.

Every ministry, every church program, every act of service should ultimately contribute to making mature disciples of Jesus Christ.

# 2\. DISCIPLES MAKE DISCIPLES

## Scripture

2 Timothy 2:2

(Read the passage.)

Paul instructed Timothy:

> "What you have heard from me... entrust to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also."

Notice the multiplication.

Paul → Timothy → Faithful People → Others

This is discipleship.

The Gospel spreads most effectively through transformed lives influencing other lives.

Every believer has learned from someone.

Perhaps:

- A parent
- A pastor
- A mentor
- A friend
- A Bible study leader

God often uses people to help people grow.

Discipleship is relational.

It involves investing in others and helping them follow Christ.

The goal is not dependency upon a leader.

The goal is maturity in Christ.

Healthy disciples eventually become disciple-makers themselves.

# 3\. SHARING THE GOSPEL

## Scripture

Romans 10:13–15

(Read the passage.)

Before people can become disciples, they must hear the Gospel.

The Gospel is good news.

It announces what God has done through Jesus Christ.

Sharing the Gospel is not the responsibility of a few gifted individuals.

It belongs to every believer.

Not everyone is called to be an evangelist.

But every disciple is called to be a witness.

Sharing the Gospel does not require perfect knowledge.

It requires faithfulness.

You can share:

- What Christ has done
- What the Gospel teaches
- How your life has changed
- Why you trust Jesus

God uses ordinary people to communicate extraordinary truth.

# 4\. THE COST AND JOY OF DISCIPLE-MAKING

## Scripture

Matthew 9:37–38

(Read the passage.)

Disciple-making is one of the greatest privileges a believer can experience.

Yet it is not always easy.

Helping people follow Jesus requires:

- Prayer
- Patience
- Time
- Commitment
- Sacrifice

People grow at different rates.

Some respond quickly.

Others take years.

Some remain faithful.

Others drift away.

Jesus experienced all of these realities in His own ministry.

Even among the Twelve, not everyone responded in the same way.

Disciple-making can be challenging.

Yet it is also deeply rewarding.

There is great joy in seeing:

- Someone come to faith
- A believer mature spiritually
- A struggling disciple restored
- A new leader emerge
- Another disciple begin making disciples

The Apostle John wrote:

> "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth."
> 
> — 3 John 4

The investment may be costly.

The reward is eternal.

# 5\. WHO SHOULD I DISCIPLE?

## Scripture

Mark 3:13–15

(Read the passage.)

Many believers ask:

"Who should I disciple?"

The answer begins with prayer.

Jesus spent time with large crowds.

He invested deeply in a smaller group.

He invested especially closely in a few individuals.

Not everyone requires the same level of attention.

Disciple-making often begins with people whom God has already placed in our lives.

Examples include:

- Family members
- Friends
- Co-workers
- Classmates
- New believers
- Younger Christians
- Ministry partners

Look for people who are:

### Available

They are willing to spend time learning and growing.

### Teachable

They are open to God's Word and correction.

### Faithful

They demonstrate commitment and consistency.

No disciple-maker can invest deeply in everyone.

Jesus Himself focused intentionally on a few.

The goal is not reaching everyone personally.

The goal is faithfully investing in those God entrusts to us.

# 6\. A SIMPLE MODEL FOR DISCIPLESHIP (F.A.I.T.H.)

## Scripture

Matthew 4:19

> "Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men."

Throughout this workbook we have emphasized that disciples make disciples.

One practical way to remember the process is through the acronym:

## F — Follow Christ

Discipleship begins with a personal relationship with Jesus.

Before we can lead others, we must first follow Him ourselves.

Questions:

- Am I walking with Christ?
- Am I obeying His Word?
- Am I growing spiritually?

Healthy discipleship begins with personal authenticity.

* * *

## A — Abide In The Word

Disciples are shaped by Scripture.

God's Word provides truth, guidance, correction, and encouragement.

Questions:

- Am I consistently reading Scripture?
- Am I applying what I learn?
- Am I helping others understand God's Word?

* * *

## I — Invest In Relationships

Discipleship is relational.

Jesus spent time with people.

He taught them, encouraged them, corrected them, and modeled life for them.

Questions:

- Who am I intentionally investing in?
- Who is investing in me?
- How can I strengthen spiritual relationships?

* * *

## T — Train Others

Discipleship involves helping others grow toward maturity.

Training includes:

- Teaching truth
- Modeling obedience
- Encouraging growth
- Providing accountability

The goal is not information alone.

The goal is transformation.

* * *

## H — Help Others Disciple Others

The process is not complete until disciples become disciple-makers.

Healthy discipleship multiplies.

A disciple who never helps others grow has not fully embraced Christ's mission.

The goal is spiritual multiplication.

One life influencing another life, which influences another life.

This is how the Gospel has spread throughout history.

* * *

# 7\. LIVING ON MISSION

## Scripture

Matthew 5:13–16

(Read the passage.)

Many Christians view mission as something that happens overseas.

The New Testament presents a broader perspective.

Every believer is called to live missionally.

Mission begins wherever God has placed us.

Your mission field may include:

- Your family
- Your workplace
- Your neighborhood
- Your school
- Your community

Jesus described His followers as:

- Salt
- Light

Salt influences its environment.

Light shines in darkness.

Believers are called to represent Christ wherever they go.

Living on mission means asking:

- How can I glorify God here?
- How can I serve others?
- How can I share Christ?
- How can I make disciples?

Mission is not merely an activity.

It is a lifestyle.

* * *

## Reflection Questions

1.  What is your current mission field?
    
2.  How can you be salt and light where God has placed you?
    
3.  What opportunities for witness already exist around you?
    
4.  What fears hinder you from living missionally?

# FINAL CHAPTER SUMMARY

Jesus' final command was clear:

Make disciples.

The mission of the Church is not merely to gather people.

It is to help people become faithful followers of Jesus Christ.

Disciple-making involves:

- Sharing the Gospel
- Teaching God's Word
- Modeling Christian living
- Encouraging spiritual growth
- Training future disciple-makers

Every believer has a role in this mission.

God has uniquely positioned each Christian to influence others.

The goal is not simply personal spiritual growth.

The goal is multiplication.

Healthy disciples become disciple-makers.

The mission continues until Christ returns.
